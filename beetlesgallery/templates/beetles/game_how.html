How the game works

In 30 seconds

  1. Pick a game. Name That Beetle: name one beetle as far down as you can (subfamily, tribe, genus, species). Family Ties: say how closely two beetles are related.
  2. Answer and keep going. Each answer brings the next beetle straight away. After each one you see what other players said about it.
  3. Unsure? Say so. “Not sure” costs almost nothing; a wrong guess costs more.
  4. Tap Exit when you are done. You see your points, and your score, level and badges update.

Points grow as you play. Your level needs both points and reliability (how often you are right). Being right on known beetles earns the most, being specific earns more than being vague, and your score never drops below zero.

Scoring

1. Beetles we already know

Some beetles in the feed have an expert-checked name. You never know which ones, so treat every beetle as if it counts.

Two photos that look alike (same photographer, place or magnification) earn up to a quarter more. A wrong answer loses 1 point for every step it is off: calling two beetles of one genus “different subfamilies” is three steps off, “same species” only one.

2. Beetles nobody has checked yet

Here there is no right answer to compare with yet, so you earn points by agreeing with strong players:

  • Only players in the better half on known beetles count as judges, and proven experts for that part of the tree count the most.
  • Agreeing with players stronger than you earns nearly full credit; agreeing with weaker players earns very little.
  • If a stronger player disagrees with you, agreeing with lots of weaker players does not help.
  • The more strong players agree with you, the more you earn, up to {{ cap }}% of what the same answer would earn on a known beetle. It never costs you points.

3. Scores keep up with the truth

When an expert later checks a beetle you answered, your answer is scored again against the real name, up or down. Points from agreement move as more people answer. This is all recalculated when you leave the game and every night.

4. Learning from mistakes

A known beetle you got wrong comes back after {{ retry_days }} days or more, marked “Seen before”. Getting it right then earns half points. Second tries help your score but not your accuracy, which only counts the first time you see a beetle.

5. Not sure?

Skipping or answering “Not sure” costs a tiny {{ unsure }} points, much less than a wrong guess. Your score never drops below zero.

Where your labels go

  • From level {{ proposal_level }}, your names for beetles nobody has checked yet go to the curators as suggestions. Curators decide.
  • Only real experts have their labels written to the database without review: when {{ min_experts }} or more experts agree on a beetle with no name yet. It is still marked unvalidated until a curator checks it.
  • You become an expert one part of the tree at a time: one subfamily, tribe or genus, after many answers there and only if you are among the most accurate players. You can be an expert on one genus and not on another, so some of your labels may skip review and others not. Your expertise tree shows where you stand.
  • Expertise is checked again all the time, so it can be lost as well as earned.

Leaderboard

The leaderboard ranks players by score, accuracy or beetles seen, all time or this week. Tap a name to see that player. The specialists board ranks players inside one subfamily, tribe or genus.
